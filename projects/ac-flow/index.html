At a glance

The paper in four results.

Can an image model learn from feedback on every step of its generation, not only on the finished picture? AC-Flow trains a small critic that scores each intermediate step of Stable Diffusion 3, and keeps it stable while the model learns online.

The problem

The reward sees only the finished image, and the obvious fix collapses.

A flow model draws an image in many small steps, yet most fine-tuning methods score only the last one. A critic could score every step, but trained naively online, it breaks.

What is missing: credit for each step, from a critic that stays stable.

The idea Step-level credit

Keep the update.
Let a critic weight each step.

Reward-weighted fine-tuning scales a flow-matching update by how good the final image was. AC-Flow replaces that one number with a weight for every step, set by the reward minus what the critic expected from that step.

Schematic, after the paper’s Fig. 1. Bars: what sets each step’s weight. In AC-Flow, a bar above the line makes a step count for more, below it for less.

Steps that beat the critic’s expectation count for more; steps that fall short count for less.

How it works

One critic, three safeguards.

The critic is small: a few convolution and MLP layers beside Stable Diffusion 3. Three safeguards make its advice safe to follow online, and a Wasserstein-2 penalty keeps the model close to where it started.

  • no image dataset
  • no likelihood
  • no hyperparameter tuning
  • no GPU cluster

A critic it can trust, and a model kept close to where it started.

The evidence Finding 01

Its critic trains without blowing up.

Plain actor-critic training on Stable Diffusion 3 never settles. Each safeguard pulls the actor loss further down; with all three, both losses come down early and stay down.

The evidence Finding 02

It keeps improving where plain actor-critic collapses.

The evidence Finding 03

It aligns best, even by judges it never saw.

Every method was fine-tuned on the same reward, CLIP score. AC-Flow scores highest on it, and on two human-preference models that played no part in its training.

DrawBench prompts on Stable Diffusion 3 (SD3, the base model, dashed). Mean and standard error over three seeds, from the paper’s Table 1.

How much variety it keeps

Diversity: the mean distance between CLIP embeddings of images for the same prompt (paper Table 1). The Wasserstein-2 penalty raises alignment and diversity together.

It learns what the prompt asks for, not a trick that pleases one reward model.

Why it matters Finding 05

Step-level feedback, for about the cost of scoring the end.

The critic adds little. Next to reward-weighted fine-tuning, a run takes about two hours and two gigabytes more, and every run fits on one GPU.

What this opens up

Fine-tuning within reach

A small critic and a light setup let online fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion 3 run on one widely available GPU.

Models that learn from their own samples

Stable online training on self-generated data points to generators that keep improving through their own outputs.

Any goal you can score

Swap in another reward, such as one for safety, and the same loop steers the model toward it.
