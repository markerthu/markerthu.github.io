At a glance

The paper in four results.

To explore, leading Atari agents such as Agent57 train a population of policies and play one of them at a time. What if every episode could blend all of them? Learnable Behavior Control does, and learns which blend to play.

The idea Blend, do not pick

Population-based agents pick one policy at a time. LBC blends them all.

Picking caps the behaviors at the number of policies, and every extra policy costs more data. A blend of the same policies can be any point in a continuous space.

{% include proj/lbc/pick.html %}

The space of behaviors grows with the blend, not with the population.

How it works

Every behavior is a blend of all its policies, and a bandit learns which blend to play.

Three policies learn side by side. Before each episode, a bandit sets a weight and a temperature for every policy. The blend plays the episode, and its return tells the bandit how good that choice was.

{% include proj/lbc/mixer.html %}

Interactive schematic: the probabilities are illustrative, not measured. Drag the point or move a slider.

1

Blend

Each policy is sharpened or flattened by its own temperature, then weighted. The weighted sum is the behavior.

2

Select

The space of blends is cut into regions. A bandit scores each by the returns it brought, plus a bonus for regions it has tried less.

3

Learn

The episodes train all three policies off-policy, and each return updates the bandit’s score for its region.

The evidence Finding 02

The highest mean score, on 1B frames.

Across all 57 games it scores highest, while training on as few frames as any agent here.

Human-normalized score sets each game’s score against a random agent and an average human player, then averages over the 57 games. Training frames: LBC and MEME 1B, Go-Explore 10B, MuZero 20B, Agent57 100B.

More score, from less experience.

The evidence Finding 03

A bigger behavior space scores higher.

Shrink the space step by step, first to one policy, then to one temperature, and the score falls each time.

Mean and median human-normalized score over the 57 games, each variant scaled to the full method; the labels are the drops the paper reports.

Enlarging the behavior space is a promising way to make existing methods stronger.

The evidence Finding 04

The blend has to be learned.

Keep the whole space but pick blends at random, and most of the score is gone.

Mean and median human-normalized score over the 57 games, scaled to the full method.

In most games, the learned choice starts with exploratory blends and turns to exploitative ones as training goes on, without letting exploration collapse to zero.

Why it matters

How an agent explores becomes something it learns.

Which behavior gathers the data used to be a fixed rule, or a pick from a fixed list. LBC turns it into a space to design and a choice to learn.

A harder yardstick

Human world records, not the average player, to claim superhuman play.

More behaviors, same policies

Blending grows the space of behaviors without training more policies.

A general recipe

It plugs into distributed off-policy agents: blend the policies, then learn the blend.
