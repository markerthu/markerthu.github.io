Self-Upgrading Embodied Agents

An embodied agent upgrades its own weights on the job: multi-round, stable, continual self-improvement, without collapse.

At a glance

The paper in four results.

We introduce the self-upgrading embodied agent, to our knowledge the first that learns from its own experience by upgrading its own weights on the job, between its tasks.

  • no training
  • no gradient
  • no reward model
  • no second model
  • no test-time scaling
  • no harness or prompt engineering

The problem A way out

An embodied agent goes to work with the weights it was given and keeps them for life.

How it works One night

By day it works. At night it rewrites one tensor of its weights.

By day the agent works in a household environment. At night it reads its record, finds many of its steps wasted on replies that reach the token limit before any action, traces the habit to one direction in its hidden states, and rewrites one tensor of its weights.

The night that kept its edit, as it happened, in 7.7 hours on the one GPU that serves it. The agent is Qwen3.5-27B with its published weights, at work in ALFWorld, a simulated household environment in text.

The evidence Finding 01

        
It is its own examiner and keeps only what holds.

        
An agent that rewrites itself must first never break itself. It tests every change against its unchanged self on practice tasks, and what does not hold is withdrawn.

      

    

    

    
Four nights in a row: one edit kept; every night that did not hold left it exactly as it was.

    
Letting an agent change its own weights is the easy part. Keeping only the changes that hold is the hard part.

  






  

    

      

        
The evidence Finding 02

        
When an edit holds, the next day it is a better agent, at the same cost per step.

        
Over thousands of held-out episodes, 48 fresh seeds a task, its one kept edit makes it complete significantly more tasks, in fewer steps, with fewer wasted and fewer tokens.

      

    

    

      

    

    
Held-out tasks, 48 fresh seeds each. All five measures were fixed in advance, and all five improve, each p < 10−4; tasks completed: +3.6 points, 95% interval +1.9 to +5.2. Whiskers: each agent’s 95% interval over the 48 seeds.

    
The more it works, the further ahead it gets

    

      

    

    
Edited minus unchanged, added up over the 48 episodes of a task: a mean over tasks, with its 95% band.

    
The same tasks, with and without its own edit

    

    
Four held-out tasks, the same seed for both agents, chosen by rule: of a task type, the task whose completed episodes rise most with the edit; of its seeds, the one the edited agent finishes in the fewest steps. ALFWorld is a household environment in text: each room is drawn from the steps of its episode.

    
An edit to the weights adds nothing to any step: an episode costs it about 8% fewer tokens.

  






  

    

      

        
The evidence Finding 03

        
Multi-round, stable and continual: the upgrade holds, without collapse.

        
The third and the fourth night started from the edit of the second, found no edit that held, and withdrew: what the second night gave it was still there on the fourth.

      

    

    

      

    

    
The agent in use after each of the four nights, on the held-out tasks. Nights 1, 3 and 4 withdrew: the level after them is not a new measurement, and those points are drawn hollow.

    
Over four nights of work on itself, the agent upgraded itself, held the upgrade through every night after it, and never collapsed.

  






  

    

      

        
The evidence Finding 04

        
The ability appears with scale.

        
Of four sizes of its family, only the 27B agent does this.

      

    

    

    
The four sizes are Qwen3.5 at 2B, 4B, 9B and 27B; the smaller models ran the same protocol.

  









  

    

      

        
What it opens

        
From here on, an embodied agent need not keep training and inference apart.

        
As it works, it gathers its own experience and upgrades itself, with no separate pre-training or post-training and no human intervention.

      

    

    

    
An illustration of what we believe this opens, not an experiment.

    
We believe this opens the door to lifelong continual learning for robots: stable, continual, recursive self-improvement.
