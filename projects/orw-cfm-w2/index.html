At a glance

The paper in four results.

Can a flow-matching image model learn from a reward alone, online, from its own samples, without collapsing? Yes: weight its own samples by their reward, and hold it near the base model with a Wasserstein‑2 bound it can compute.

The problem

Reward fine-tuning works for diffusion models. For flow matching, three things stand in the way.

Policy gradients

The likelihood is too costly.

Methods such as DDPO need each sample's likelihood. For a continuous flow, that means integrating the model's divergence along its whole path.

Offline re-weighting

A fixed dataset stops short.

Re-weighting data collected in advance cannot reach the best policy: the online–offline gap. DPO also needs pairs and filtered data.

Online, unchecked

Learning online collapses.

A model that keeps training on its own best samples narrows until it makes one image: the over-optimization seen in diffusion models.

What is missing: an online method with a limit that a flow model can actually compute.

The finding Lemma 1

Learning online reaches the best reward. Left unchecked, it collapses.

We prove that re-weighting its own samples round after round sharpens the model toward the highest reward, until it makes the same image every time. The usual limit, a KL penalty, needs the very likelihood a flow cannot afford.

Schematic of the paper's analysis (Theorem 2, Lemma 1 and Theorem 5), not measured. Bars: share of samples in each of five modes; dashed: the base model.

So keep the online loop, and add a limit the model can compute.

How it works

Learn from its own samples, within reach of where it started.

Each round it weights its own samples by their reward and trains on them. A Wasserstein‑2 bound, computed from the two models' vector fields, keeps it near the base model.

  • no likelihoods
  • no reward gradients
  • no filtered datasets
  • no human-collected data
Reward-weighted flow matching

Trains on its own samples, each weighted by its reward. Online, the weights compound round after round.

τ sets how fast it converges; α sets where it settles.

Why it matters

Change the reward, not the dataset.

It needs only a score for each image it makes: no human‑collected data, no filtered datasets, no gradient of the reward.

Stable Diffusion 3, “a train on top of a surfboard”, with three other reward models

HPS-V2

Paper, Fig. 9, in the paper’s order.

Any score will do

A classifier, a JPEG file size, CLIP, HPS‑V2, Pick Score, Alpha Clip: one method, no reward‑specific changes.

Its own data

Every training image is one it generated itself. Flip the sign of the reward and the same model learns odd digits instead of even ones.

Two dials, clear roles

τ sets how fast it converges and α where it settles, so fine-tuning stays under control.
